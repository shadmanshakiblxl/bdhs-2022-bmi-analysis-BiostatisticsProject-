01 / Executive Overview

The Dual Burden of Nutritional Transition

Key Research Premise

Body Mass Index ($\text{BMI}$) reflects the complex intersection between macroeconomic development, food security, and urban lifestyle shifts in South Asia.

Body Mass Index serves as a fundamental metric for assessing population-level health transitions in developing economies. Utilizing nationally representative secondary data from the Bangladesh Demographic and Health Survey (BDHS) 2022, this empirical study evaluates how macro-level socio-demographic drivers systematically shape body mass profiles among adults aged 15–49 years .

Out of the total weighted sample of $N = 9,946$ respondents, 53.2% maintained a Normal BMI ($18.5 \le \text{BMI} \le 24.9 \, \text{kg/m}^2$), whereas 46.8% exhibited heightened nutritional risk (falling into either underweight or overweight/obese classifications) .

Bivariate hypothesis testing ($\alpha = 0.05$) demonstrates that structural socioeconomic drivers—specifically Wealth Index Quintiles ($\chi^2 = 168.099, p < 0.001$), Type of Residence ($\chi^2 = 67.738, p < 0.001$), and Educational Attainment ($\chi^2 = 24.949, p < 0.001$)—act as the principal determinants of body mass outcomes, vastly outperforming household headship sex or working status .

03 / Key Determinants

Socio-Demographic Drivers vs. Invariant Controls

Comparative breakdown between statistically significant determinants ($\alpha = 0.05$) and invariant household control variables tested across the sample .

Significant Drivers ($p < 0.001$)

4 Factors
Wealth Index Quintiles $\chi^2 = 168.099$

Strongest overall predictor of BMI variation. While 60.0% of the poorest quintile maintain normal weight, 58.1% of the richest quintile fall into nutritional risk (driven by urban overweight trends) .

Type of Residence $\chi^2 = 67.738$

Rural residents exhibit higher normal BMI prevalence (56.2% vs. 43.8% at-risk). Conversely, urban residents display an inverted risk profile (52.4% at-risk vs. 47.6% normal) .

Administrative Division $\chi^2 = 26.325$

Metropolitan administrative hubs (Dhaka & Chattogram) carry elevated non-communicable disease risk, whereas agrarian divisions (Mymensingh: 57.6% normal) retain higher baseline normality .

Educational Level $\chi^2 = 24.949$

Higher education correlates with elevated body mass shifts (49.9% at-risk), mediated by sedentary white-collar employment and dietary shifts toward processed calorie intake .

Non-Significant Controls ($p > 0.05$)

2 Controls
Current Working Status $\chi^2 = 3.662 \mid p = 0.056$

Marginal difference between working (54.6% normal) and non-working (52.6% normal) respondents. The relationship did not cross the significance threshold at $\alpha = 0.05$ .

Sex of Household Head $\chi^2 = 0.699 \mid p = 0.403$

Male-headed households (85.2% of sample) and female-headed households (14.8%) yielded virtually identical proportional BMI distributions, ruling out headship sex as an independent predictor .

Methodological takeaway

Household power structures (headship gender) and individual employment status exert negligible influence on adult BMI compared to macro structural variables like wealth tier and urban geography .

06 / Policy & Research

Strategic Public Health Recommendations

Translating empirical biostatistical findings into targeted national public health policy for Bangladesh .

01

Dual-Target Health Strategy

Public health frameworks must pivot away from "one-size-fits-all" programs. Policy should maintain rural food security programs to combat undernutrition while implementing urban lifestyle awareness campaigns targeting overnutrition in affluent wealth tiers .

02

WHO Standard Disaggregation

Future epidemiological research should replace binary BMI groupings with full WHO 4-category classification (Underweight, Normal, Overweight, Obese) to explicitly isolate underweight cohorts from overweight/obese populations .

03

Multivariable Confounder Control

Future frameworks should execute multivariable ordinal logistic regression models to control for co-dependent socio-demographic confounders, such as the inherent collinearity between wealth index and educational level .
